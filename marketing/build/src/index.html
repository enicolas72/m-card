How it works

Tag. Drop. Play.

01

Tag

Every album gets its own M-Card: the cover art on top, an NFC chip inside. Print your own, then pair each one from the built-in web console in seconds.

02

Drop

Slide the card into the slot, cover facing out. Or slip it inside a record sleeve and simply stand the vinyl in the slot. The reader in the backrest picks it up the moment it lands.

03

Play

Roon starts the album on your zone: any DAC, any speaker, bit-perfect. The display shows what's playing; three steel buttons take care of the rest.

Put the same card back? Nothing restarts. Pull it out? The music keeps going. M-Card just does what you'd expect.

The display

A screen with soul.

A 20×4 backlit character LCD with the same dot-matrix glow as the hi-fi decks you grew up with. Long titles scroll, the playback state sits in the corner, and the backlight dims itself when the room goes quiet.

The console

Runs in any browser.
Nothing to install.

M-Card serves its own web page on your local network: live status, logs, configuration, and a library search that pairs a card to any Roon album in one click.

mcard.local:8088/search?q=jarre

The actual M-Card web interface, as served by the device.

Hardware

Built, not bought.

Open hardware you can build in a weekend: off-the-shelf parts, no soldering on the reader, and a 3D-printed enclosure that prints without supports.

Your records become the remote.

Slip an M-Card inside a vinyl sleeve or a CD case and the record itself becomes the trigger: stand it in the slot and Roon plays that very album, in full quality, on your system. Your sleeves and your hi-fi, finally working together.

Self-healing

Roon restarts, Wi-Fi drops: M-Card reconnects on its own. No reboot, ever.

Forgiving

Album titles are matched despite case, accents, punctuation or a stray “Vol.”.

Private by design

It talks to your Roon server and nothing else. No cloud, no telemetry.

Zero-friction pairing

Unknown card? It's queued automatically, then paired from the console with one click.
